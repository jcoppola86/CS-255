- Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

DriverPass wanted a system that would help students prepare for their DMV driving tests. The system needed to let customers purchase training packages, schedule driving lessons, take practice tests, and keep track of their progress. Employees also needed to be able to manage customer accounts, appointments, and update DMV information when changes were made. Throughout this course, I designed the system from the planning stage by creating the business requirements document, system design document, and UML diagrams that showed how everything would work together.

- What did you do particularly well?

Looking back, I’m probably most proud of my UML diagrams because those were the part of the project I spent the most time improving. They definitely didn’t come easy at first, but each assignment helped me understand UML a little better. I also learned that small details matter, especially with things like relationships, notation, and keeping the diagrams organized. After getting feedback throughout the course, I started explaining my decisions more instead of just answering the questions, and I think my later assignments were much stronger because of it.

- If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could go back and change one thing, I would probably revise my class diagram one more time. Even though I’m happy with where it ended up, I understand it much better now than I did when I first created it. I think I could simplify a few areas and make some of the relationships a little clearer so someone seeing the project for the first time could understand it more quickly.

- How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?

For me, everything came back to what the client asked for during the DriverPass interview. Whenever I updated a diagram or document, I went back and checked the original requirements to make sure I wasn’t leaving anything out. I also thought about what each type of user would actually need to do in the system, whether it was a customer scheduling a lesson or an employee managing appointments. If the design doesn’t meet the user’s needs, then it doesn’t really matter how well the system is built because it won’t solve the problem it was created for.

- How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

One thing this course taught me is that it’s much easier to design a system when you break it into smaller pieces instead of trying to figure everything out at once. Working through the requirements first and then building diagrams one at a time helped me stay organized and catch mistakes earlier. In the future, I would continue gathering as much information as possible before starting the design, ask questions whenever something isn’t clear, and keep checking my work against the original requirements. I learned that spending more time planning in the beginning usually saves time later because there are fewer changes to make during development.
